# The Oversight — Corruption Canon (Steve's design, 2026-10-09)

Corruption is the dark mirror of trust. Trust is per-relationship and
progressive (higher is harder to earn). Corruption is per-character, 0-100:
it measures what you've become.

## The law

- **The number never shows.** "If you don't know, it doesn't show" applies
  to corruption absolutely. Others sense it through behavior tells, witnessed
  acts, and unease — never a meter.
- **Foil:** high corruption drags trust gains. The two systems push against
  each other: the more corrupt you are, the harder trust is to earn.
- **Fear is not trust.** Fear compels obedience but never loyalty. Fearful
  villagers comply (intimidation works on anyone they dread) but they don't
  love, don't fight at your side, and their fear collapses into contempt the
  moment you show weakness.
- **Evil is a legitimate path to power** (Steve 2026-10-09, overruling the
  earlier "cut it if evil feels rewarding" line). In a corrupted village it's
  the quickest one. The terrible bargain stays: real power at a real price —
  cravings, brittle loyalty, exile risk, prion death.
- **Televised.** This is an alien entertainment show. Villains are good TV.
  The System notices dark runs: audience reactions, gambler chatter, dark-lane
  fans sending ominous care packages.

## The slope (cannibalism)

Butcher a person-corpse → human meat → eat. Each step is honest and awful:

- **First meal:** devastating trauma (~35), big corruption jump (+14).
- **Desensitization:** each repeat costs less trauma (×0.82 per meal, floor 6)
  while corruption gains accelerate (+3 per meal, cap 30). Easier to do,
  harder to stop — that's the horror.
- **Cravings:** at 60+ corruption the hunger has a voice. Three days without
  feeding it costs energy every dawn. At 85+ it's worse.
- **Prions:** 15% per meal contracts Trembles (mundane pool, kuru analogue).
  Cooking doesn't kill prions. Nothing does. No cure at any tier.
- **Witnessed vs hidden:** witnessed acts detonate socially immediately;
  hidden acts feed only corruption + trauma — but the crime stays on the books
  (witnessed:false) for the detective/moot path. Discovery later detonates
  retroactively.

## Contextual consequences (Steve: "In a normal village, cannibalism is exile.
In a starving or corrupted village — it's the quickest source of power.")

Consequences are contextual, never fixed. The question the code answers is
"what would this village tolerate today," from existing state:

- **Healthy village → exile.** Discovered cannibalism fast-tracks the moot;
  the charge outranks murder. Exile, not a trust hit.
- **Starving village → horror without exile.** Sustained food stress / recent
  starvation deaths erode norms. The act still horrifies; the village lacks
  the strength for outrage, let alone exile.
- **Corrupted village → power move.** Bottom-up village corruption ≥45 (or a
  corrupt figurehead) inverts the norm: witnessed cannibalism breeds dread,
  dread is authority, and the corrupt approve (shared secrets bond).

## Villagers are full participants (Steve 2026-10-09)

- Corruption is not player-only. Villagers accumulate it from their own acts
  (cannibalism, betrayal, murder — witnessed or hidden) with the same
  desensitization mechanics.
- Village corruption emerges **bottom-up** from members' corruption, not a flag.
- Corrupt villagers tempt/pressure the player: offers of meat, witnessed acts
  that normalize (thinning the player's own line), social cover (the corrupt
  don't judge — they recognize).
- In starving villages, desperate corrupt villagers may cross the line on
  their own — leaving evidence the player can discover.
- Same tone law for NPC arcs: awful and consequential, never cartoon villainy.

## Psycho spawns (Steve 2026-10-09)

Character generation may rarely make the PLAYER the dark one: picking (or
inheriting, on mantle-passing) a villager with a malicious hidden trait fires
a psycho bundle — flashback, inner voice, starting corruption 20, early tells
surfaced. The backstory explains the trait (generator coherence — no random
labels). The fiction acknowledges what you are without handing you a villain
manual. The fun is the role, not a power trip.
