# VISION — The Scattering

> Working title. A system-apocalypse survival roguelite for mobile PWA.

## Thesis

**No matter how much fantasy or sci-fi the game accrues, it never stops being a gritty long haul of survival logistics: feeding yourself, hunting game, obtaining clean water.** Food is a real scarcity. Power never exempts you from hunger — it raises the metabolic price of it.

## Pillars

1. **Hunger is the final boss.** The calorie economy is the master clock. A max-level character still needs ~2,500 kcal/day. The UI's largest element is always the hunger/water meter, at every level.
2. **The System gives everything except survival.** Combat skills, stat boosts, flashy abilities — handed out freely. Foraging, cooking, water purification: learnable only from the world (books, NPCs, trial and error, the Codex). The aliens "forgot" the basics, and the game enforces it mechanically.
3. **Escalation taxes survival.** Every fantasy/sci-fi escalation must make dinner harder, not easier — corrupted zones poison forage, monsters disrupt game migration, System storms spoil stores. If a new mechanic makes survival easier, it gets cut or rebalanced.
4. **Knowledge is the progression; levels are the decoration.** The Codex (plants, monsters, recipes, terrain) persists across deaths. RNG controls circumstances, never competence.
5. **People, not classes.** Villagers are individuals with System-assessed ability kits drawn from pools, weighted by personality and situation. Backstories are UI — you read the person to intuit the kit.
6. **The village lives.** It simulates on the expedition clock (1 expedition day = 1 village day): mouths eat, moods shift, projects progress, events fire. Time pauses between sessions — attachment without guilt.
7. **Every life answers the same question.** Each scholar's run is a different answer to "what are we eating this week." Granted identity → tested identity → chosen identity.

## Fiction spine

The System (alien integration framework) scattered humanity into villages across twisted Earth. It grants combat and utility abilities freely but "forgot" survival basics — no foraging skill tree exists. One villager holds the **Codex skill**; each player run is one of its **designated scholars**, sent out to keep the village fed and the Codex growing. Scholars die; the village endures; the Codex remembers.

Campaign win: keep the village alive through its first full year **and** uncover why the aliens forgot the survival basics. Thematic close: **you win when the village no longer needs heroes.** Endless mode follows.

## Tone

Gritty, warm, wry. The System speaks in cold alien assessment-voice. The village speaks like people. The Codex-holder remembers every scholar who didn't come back.
