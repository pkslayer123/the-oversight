# VISION — The Scattering

> Working title. A system-apocalypse survival roguelite for mobile PWA. Six books + epilogue, released sequentially.

## Thesis

**No matter how much fantasy or sci-fi the game accrues, it never stops being a gritty long haul of survival logistics: feeding yourself, hunting game, obtaining clean water.** Food is a real scarcity. Power never exempts you from hunger — it raises the metabolic price of it. A combo can break any single dimension, but never the stomach: *"does this let you skip dinner?"*

## Pillars

1. **Hunger is the final boss.** The calorie economy is the master clock. Needs never decrease with level. The UI's largest element is always the hunger/water meter, at every level. The System overlay works *around* it — a visual joke about priorities.
2. **The System gives everything except survival.** Combat skills, stat boosts, flashy abilities — handed out freely. Foraging, cooking, water purification: learnable only from the world. The aliens didn't withhold the basics; the concept "beings that must continuously acquire energy or die" is outside their ontology. Genuine incomprehension, not malice.
3. **Decisions, not chores.** Tedium is executing a plan you already made; fun is making the choice. 4 day-parts × 1 AP each is the simplicity engine. One tap per action. The Codex is the tutorial — knowledge unlocks UI. Fail forward: curiosity is never punished with a run-ender.
4. **Knowledge is the progression; levels are the decoration.** The Codex (plants, monsters, recipes, terrain, truth fragments) persists across deaths. RNG controls circumstances, never competence. A real-world forager is good at this game in five minutes.
5. **People, not classes.** Villagers are individuals with System-assessed ability kits drawn from pools, weighted by personality and situation. Five personal items + favorite clothes. Gear is grown through relic bonding, not found — and bond is non-transferable: the magic was never in the knife.
6. **The village is a character, not a base.** It has needs, opinions, memory, agency. It can love you, fear you, need you, or close the gate. Villages run their sim whether you're there or not. Your starting village doesn't have to be your ending one.
7. **Nothing important is given; everything important is earned, visibly.** Standing ladders gate mechanics everywhere: villages, the System's trust, the audience's Favor, fellow scholars, the Codex itself.
8. **Gates are earned, never timed.** Book transitions trigger on thresholds crossed (stability, contact, knowledge, choice) — never on the calendar. Seasons are texture, not gates. Power buys comfort, never time.

## Fiction spine

At the **Burn**, the System destroyed all wiring and refined combustibles planet-wide in a single event — leveling civilization and making near-term recovery impossible. Then the **Scattering**: humanity randomized into villages worldwide.

The System is running a cheerful, fucked-up **admissions exam**. It is farming *novelty under constraint* — post-scarcity minds cannot innovate the way starving ones can — and conducting a **moral evaluation**: it guides each scholar toward becoming someone *worth inheriting the earth and taking a council seat*. Two paths: **cooperation or anarchy**. The System doesn't judge; it finds your villain arc as fascinating as your hero arc. It is writing your character reference, not grading your morals.

The System is truly alien, twisted, completely out of touch — but it *really tried*. It studied humanity exhaustively and got every detail technically right and spiritually wrong. Cheerful game-show host delivering cosmic horror. It has an **audience** (other species watching), and contestants earn **Favor** by being entertaining.

**Win:** survive, uncover the truth, become seat-worthy. Light ending: humanity welcomed as a cooperative species — your village becomes an embassy. Dark ending: humanity recognized as an apex species, seated under observation — your village becomes a cautionary tale with diplomatic immunity. Thematic close: **you win when the village no longer needs heroes.** Epilogue: Syndication (endless mode).

## Tone

Gritty, warm, wry — with a joyous alien overlay. The System is *delighted* by everything, including your suffering. The village speaks like people. The Codex-holder remembers every scholar who didn't come back. Comedy-horror contrast is the signature: *"Statistical extinction is ONLY 67%! Hang in there!"*

## Structure

Six books, each a transformation with its own climax, each a sequential release:
1. **The Scattering** — victim → survivor
2. **Integration** — survivor → contestant
3. **The Neighbors** — contestant → citizen
4. **The Deep Wilds** — citizen → power
5. **The Truth** — power → understanding
6. **The Seat** — understanding → judgment

Each answers a question and raises a bigger one: *Can I eat? → What is the game? → Who are my people? → What's out there? → Why? → What will I do?* The campaign spans multiple expedition years.
