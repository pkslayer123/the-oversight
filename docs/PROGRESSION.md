# The Progression — design authority

**Status:** design authority for the next build phase, 2026-10-10. Steve's
decisions below are settled law. Open calls are marked [NEEDS-STEVE].

Companion docs: MONSTER-WAVES.md (wave law, roster), SCALE.md (the ladder as
built), CONTESTS.md (the show). This doc is the *why and what next* — the
shape of a ~100-day game.

---

## Settled law (Steve, 2026-10-10)

**1. The win target is ~100 days.** A strong run should win around day 90–115.
Not 200, not 47. Pacing is reactive — kill counts and deeds are the long
pole, never calendar scripts.

**2. The table demands every wave.** `deedGateReady()`: 5/5/4/3/2 DISTINCT
monsters fought blow-by-blow per wave 1–5, plus 3+ contests survived, national+
scale, 3+ crises weathered, sentiment taught, feast surge used, stage ≥ 3.
**Knowledge never gates** — regional knowledge differs, and gating on it would
lock whole regions out of finishing. (Pacing audit: strong run unlocks wave 5
~day 77–93, faces 2+ Producers ~day 83–99. Evidence: prog-deed-gate-retune.md.)

**3. Waves 3–5 are built.** 26 monsters as data (56 total): The Final Draft
(w3), The Mirror Draft (w4), The Producers (w5) — including Steve's THE EATER,
which grows stronger with the calories it consumes (the Living Garden's
predator, the dark mirror of the food thesis). Numbers anchored to *measured*
godhood DPS (~220/round), not guesses — the draft's bands would have died in
two rounds. First real `pierce` assignments. **Gating is reactive engagement +
scale, never calendar alone** (balance pass 2026-10-10: the kill-only lane
never fired in organic play — players flee, they don't farm. Unlocks now take
kills OR engagement: w2 = day 8 + (4 w1 kills OR 2 distinct w1 faced); w3 =
day 25 + (8 w2 kills OR 2 distinct w2 faced); w4/w5 = kill/engagement lane +
regional/national scale. Kills are the faster lane, not the only lane; the
5/5/4/3/2 table bars are unchanged). **SUPERSEDED 2026-10-10 by the Wave
Ledger (section 12 below, Steve's reversal of 8730921c): unlocks are
kills-only on a point ledger — bars {1:5,2:5,3:4,4:3,5:2} as point
thresholds, per-type cap 2. Engagements still feed the ENDGAME deed gate
(5/5/4/3/2), which is unchanged.** **Villager experiences count the same**
(Steve 2026-10-10): a villager's blow-by-blow fight (fight, survive, flee, or
kill) feeds the engagement lanes and the deed bars exactly like the player's
— the village is the protagonist. **(Ledger update 2026-10-10: "the engagement
lanes" no longer exist for unlocks — a villager's fight feeds the ledger ONLY
through the kill, exactly like the player's. Fights/survives/flees still feed
the deed bars, unchanged.)** Definitions NOT weakened: same bars, same
distinct-monster counts, keyed by monster type (villager + player facing the
same hushwolf = one count). The dead told no tale — a villager who DIES in
the fight records nothing; corpses and pre-contact evades record nothing.
Fleeing counts, same as the player. Doom deaths trigger
phoenix/second_wind like any death —
"unkillable means unkillable."

**4. The scale ladder is felt moments.** Village → regional (first link, the
Regional Dawn) → national (the polity moment) → global (The Watchers) → the
table. National must offer MORE than lead-vs-belong: lead a hegemony, join as
valued subordinate, **confederate as equals**, **build a trade league**,
**conquer**, or **refuse the scale and pay for it**. (The built version only
has lead/belong — broadening is open work.)

**5. The density fiction.** ~460M Havens of 12, ~500m apart on habitable land.
Steve's population rule: children ineligible; only ~half of adults "deemed
interesting enough" — the rest erased. Villages are NEAR and contact happens
EARLY. The old isolation/quarantine idea is dead — Steve rejected it as
game-breaking for foraging. Inter-village cooperation matters from the start.

**6. Other villages are other players.** They run the same game: forage,
deplete, fight, politick, splinter — a cannibal faction can split and half
petition to join *you*. Never RNG husks. The player's interface stays narrow:
**max ~3 named faces per village** (speaker, leader, champion/rival);
everything else arrives via the rumor pipeline. Camera rule: you manage ONE
village; the world reaches you through people.

**7. Parties are capped; armies are events.** Expeditions cap at ~4–6. But
occasional big fights and raised armies are allowed — as *costly events*:
raising an army pulls workers off food, requires villager buy-in (that's
within-village drama, automatically), and answers specific threats. **A solo
village cannot survive late waves alone** — coalition-or-death is the
late-game engine, not a points ladder.

**8. Monsters are party-aware.** Bigger, badder, lethal — designed *for*
parties: pack hunters and party-splitters, not flat bigger numbers. Lethality
must threaten groups without deleting solo foragers outright. Static numbers
get trivialized by 24-person parties and punish lone scouts; tactics scale,
stats don't.

**9. Depletion is real.** Foraging grounds deplete; foraging alone can't carry
a village. This is what makes fishing, trapping, and farming builds NECESSARY
— and what manufactures village-to-village drama (trade, territory, raids) out
of your own village's needs. The drama is always *about* your people.

**10. The village grows: 12 → 24.** Through **resource thresholds**
(Steve 2026-10-10), each tier visibly improving the Haven and granting new
abilities; the System announces the next tier and the Haven shows a progress
meter, so the path is unmissable.

**11. Meta progression exists.** 45 cross-run achievements, persistent Records
screen on the title, zero leakage into new games. New games start fresh; the
record is the replay incentive. (Built 2026-10-10, evidence:
prog-meta-progression.md.)

## Open questions, answered with mechanisms

### When a monster shows up at your door, how do you call for help?

Four tiers, each with honest costs. Help is never free and never guaranteed.

1. **Runner.** Send a villager sprinting to the nearest linked village. Cost:
   the runner (time + real danger — they can die on the road), and time
   (distance in day-parts, never instant). Fastest for close neighbors,
   useless if the road is hot.
2. **Signal fire / smoke.** Light the beacon. Visible to every village in
   range — including hostile ones and every monster between. It's fast and
   loud and *indiscriminate*: raiders read smoke too. Using it is a decision
   with a downside, not a button.
3. **System relay (integration-gated).** At high integration the System will
   carry a call — but the System is enthusiastic and misunderstands urgency;
   the message arrives *translated*, possibly wrong, and the System takes a
   cut (a favor owed, narrated aloud). Reliable-ish, costly, alien.
4. **Ability cries.** Specific abilities (shouts, flares, beast-calls) punch
   through — earned, build-gated, the min-maxer's answer.

**Who answers:** linked villages with standing ≥ the ask, weighed by their own
situation — a village mid-crisis can't come, and the game says so. Their help
is a party (capped like yours), led by one of their named faces. **Cost to
them:** their foragers leave their fields — they will remember it, in trust
or in tribute owed. **If nobody comes:** the game says so aloud, fast — no
silent waiting. The refusal itself is drama (and gossip).

### Remote communication by tier

- **Village:** runners and smoke. That's it. Distance is real.
- **Regional:** the rumor pipeline formalizes — traders carry messages weekly
  (delayed, possibly wrong). Signal chains: linked villages relay beacons,
  each hop adding delay and distortion. Gossip mechanics already do this.
- **National:** polity logistics — the System's governance layer routes
  messages with tribute grain (×1.25 already exists for grain; messages ride
  the same rails). A polity has a *cadence*: weekly court news, not instant
  chat.
- **Global:** the world feed — the galaxy's cameras see everything and the
  chat talks. You don't message the world; the world messages you.

**The switchboard is an OFFICE, not a god path** (Steve 2026-10-10). The
deliberate choice is *who you lose to the relay*: every candidate costs
something different (your best talker weakens your moots; your weakest
forager breeds resentment at the appointment). The appointment is a public,
televised beat — the System names them, the village reacts. The officeholder
routes every message and can prioritize, delay, or *edit* — the corruption
surface is the point. Their mini-progression rides the game's own ladder
(village relay → regional switchboard → national voice → a name the planet
knows), never a skill tree. What they do with the messages feeds the ending
frame. Build tiers 1–4 of the call-for-help chain first; the office arrives
with the regional game.

### The 24-cap: growth milestones

Growth is **resource-based** (Steve 2026-10-10): hitting a stockpile
threshold raises the Haven to the next tier, each tier visibly improving the
Haven and granting new abilities. Deed-gating was rejected — but
discoverability is the risk, so the path is unmissable: **the System
announces the next tier on screen** ("store 2,000 kcal and 50 wood to raise
the Longhouse" — dangling upgrades is exactly what the System does for
ratings), backed by a visible progress meter on the Haven and villagers
gossiping about what's needed. No tutorial popups, no hidden paths.

Tiers (threshold numbers to be tuned by playtest):

- **16 — The Longhouse.** Stockpile bar (food + wood). Unlocks: the
  longhouse (hearth upgrades, winter food bonus), +4 population cap.
- **20 — The Palisade.** Stockpile bar (food + wood + stone). Unlocks:
  palisade (raid defense, safe sleep), +4 cap.
- **24 — The Granary.** Stockpile bar (food + preserved stores). Unlocks:
  granary (spoilage slows, famine buffer), +4 cap.

**Refugees, petitioners, and splinter groups** are how the cap gets *filled*:
a cannibal faction's breakaways petitioning at your fire don't just vanish
into the population number — they're named, interviewed, voted on (that's a
moot, that's drama). The cap is the ceiling; the village decides who stands
under it.

### How the camera rule survives the big game

The guardrail is a number: **your village (up to 24, deep) + at most a
handful of named outsiders (shallow but consistent)**. Mechanisms that hold
it:

- **Representatives, always.** Every inter-village beat goes through the ~3
  faces. You never open "the region screen" — you talk to *their speaker*.
- **Rumor filter.** If a system needs you to know a 4th outsider's name, it
  goes through gossip instead — distorted, delayed, deniable.
- **Armies are yours.** A raised army is *your* villagers plus allied parties
  under *your* banner. You command people you know; allies arrive as named
  parties, not rosters.
- **The table is personal.** The endgame scenes are conversations, not
  summits — the scale is felt through who sits across from you, not how many
  chairs.

The moment the player is tracking 40 characters, the 24 stop mattering. Any
system that asks for that gets redesigned or cut.

## 12. The Wave Ledger (Steve, 2026-10-10 — settled same turn)

Wave unlocks run on **KILLS ONLY** — a per-wave, per-village, per-run point
ledger, cumulative across player deaths (it lives on `state`, not the
scholar; a new adventurer inherits the village's ledger).

- **Kill = `pointsPerKill`** (default 1). **Counter-kill =
  `pointsPerCounterKill`** (default 2) — a kill executed with the monster's
  *discovered* counter (mastery bonus). The convention
  (`Game.monsterCounterKnown`): `state.monsterCounters[id] === true` AND the
  monster def carries a `counter` field. Verified 2026-10-10: NO monster def
  has counter data yet — the bonus is **dormant** (the parallel
  signature-mechanics loop will adopt the convention; until then kills never
  score less than 1, never punished).
- **Per monster-type cap `perTypeCap`** (default 2) per wave — breadth, not
  farming. The 3rd kill of the same type in the same wave scores 0.
- **Bars `barsPerWave` = {"1":5,"2":5,"3":4,"4":3,"5":2}** — Steve's settled
  bars, now POINT thresholds. To unlock wave N+1, wave N's ledger must reach
  barsPerWave[N]. **This is the pacing dial Steve will turn** ("It doesn't
  sound like this will scale for a 100 day game. But write the system and we
  can turn the rest of the levers." — calibration comes later, via sims).
- **Day floors:** wave 2 needs day >= `wave2DayFloor` (8); waves 3+ need day
  >= `dayFloor` (25). Floors, never scripts.
- **Scale gates unchanged:** wave 4 needs scaleRank >= 'regional', wave 5 >=
  'national' (`Game.scaleAtLeast`; absent scaleRank defaults 'regional').
- The levers live in **`src/data/wave-ledger.json`** (loaded by `Game.init`,
  `this.data.waveLedger`); the engine reads them through
  `Game.waveLedgerCfg()` with in-code defaults, so a missing/stale file can
  never break a kill. Module: `src/js/waveLedger.js` (loads after
  progression.js). The System televises progress: `sysSay` beats at 50% and
  100% of each wave's bar, idempotent via `state._ledgerAnn50/_ledgerAnnFull`;
  one persistent progress line in `beatsRowHTML()` with honest labels
  ("kills only").

### Reversal of 8730921c (2026-10-10, Steve's call)

The bal-waves engagement lanes (commit 8730921c, morning 2026-10-10) are
**reversed for unlocks**: villager wave experiences no longer count toward
unlocking waves. **Engagements (faced/fled) score ZERO on the ledger.**
Villager **kills** still count — the village is the protagonist, but only
through kills. What this means for the settled law in item 3 above: the
"engagement lanes" paragraph is superseded; the endgame **deed gate**
(5/5/4/3/2 distinct faced, `deedGateReady()`) is **untouched** — engagements
still feed the deed bars, they just don't unlock waves. Tests updated
honestly: `test-bal-waves`, `test-villager-wave-xp`, `test-wave3-5` now
assert ledger-only gates; the engagement-feed assertions remain for the
deed gate.

### Every lever, and what turning it does

| Lever | Default | Turning it up | Turning it down |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pointsPerKill` | 1 | kill throughput; waves unlock faster per body | slower unlocks per kill; counter bonus matters relatively more |
| `pointsPerCounterKill` | 2 | mastery reward weight — rewards learning counters (dormant until counter data ships) | flattens mastery (1 = no bonus at all) |
| `perTypeCap` | 2 | lets players farm one monster type longer (weaker breadth pressure) | forces breadth: 1 = every kill must be a new type (hardest) |
| `barsPerWave` | {5,5,4,3,2} | THE pacing dial: higher bars delay every wave's unlock, stretching the 100-day game | lower bars accelerate the escalation; 1 = nearly instant |
| `dayFloor` / `wave2DayFloor` | 25 / 8 | calendar floors rise — unlocks can't arrive before the floor no matter the kills | floors drop — reactive kills can unlock waves very early |
| `counterBonusEnabled` (per wave) | all true | kill-switch for the mastery bonus: false on a wave means counter kills score 1 there | (same dial, per-wave granularity) |
